The RoPE ablation — Finding 5's mechanism is REAL, and it is NOT most of the gap
================================================================================
Run 2026-08-19, 22:20-23:55. Harness: scripts/run_rope_ablation.py
Data:    stress_results/rope_ablation_block.csv (24 runs)
Console: artifacts/rope_ablation/block.log    Logs: artifacts/rope_ablation/logs/
Code:    llama.cpp/src/llama-kv-cache.cpp, llama_kv_cache::update(), the
         UNIKV_NO_REENCODE gate.

HEADLINE
--------
The K-shift re-encode is a real, measurable cost and it scales exactly as the
"it re-encodes the whole cache" story predicts — 0.92 ms per shift at C=1024 and
1.81 ms at C=2048, a ratio of 1.96 against a predicted 2.00.

But at C=1024, the budget the paper actually quotes, it accounts for only 35.4%
of the policy-1-to-H2O gap. Nearly two thirds of that gap is something else.

So Finding 5 names the right mechanism and overstates its share. The sentence
"the rolling window costs more because llama.cpp re-encodes rotary embeddings
across the cache" is not wrong about the cause but is wrong about the
accounting, and at the headline budget it is wrong by a factor of about three.

THE INSTRUMENT
--------------
UNIKV_NO_REENCODE=1 gates the K-shift pass inside llama_kv_cache::update():

    if (hparams.rope_type != LLAMA_ROPE_TYPE_NONE && !unikv_no_reencode) {
        ggml_backend_sched_reset(sched);
        ... build_graph_shift(); ggml_backend_sched_alloc_graph();
            res->set_inputs(); lctx->graph_compute();
    }
    for (...) cells.reset_shift();     // still runs in BOTH cases

Precisely what is removed: the entire K-shift graph pass — build, allocate, set
inputs and compute — not merely the rotary arithmetic inside it. What is kept:
seq_add's position renumbering, the cell scan, the head fix-up, the eviction
itself and reset_shift(). The difference between the arms is therefore the cost
of the whole shift pass, and the analysis should say "the K-shift pass" wherever
it is tempting to say "the rope kernel".

THE ABLATED ARM PRODUCES WRONG OUTPUT, BY CONSTRUCTION. Surviving cells keep
positions that no longer match their rotary encoding. This is an instrument, not
a policy, and none of its text is reported as a quality result.

INSTRUMENT VALIDATION (before the block, artifacts/rope_ablation/, /tmp probes)
  - the banner "RoPE re-encode SKIPPED" appears in the ablated arm's own log and
    in no other arm's: asserted per run in BOTH directions by the harness, 6/6
    and 18/18 across the block, zero flags.
  - with a 512-token prompt at C=1024 the ablated arm's sampled token ids are
    IDENTICAL to the normal arm's through index 514 and first differ at index
    515 — exactly the first cache overflow (512 prompt + 512 generated = 1024).
    Before any shift the two are the same computation; from the first shift they
    diverge. That is the signature of a gate that touches the shift path and
    nothing else.
  - the default path is provably untouched: the flag defaults false and the
    condition is `&& !unikv_no_reencode`.

  (A first smoke test of mine was invalid and is recorded here so the validation
  is not overstated: 512 prompt + 64 generated at C=1024 never fills the cache,
  so no shift occurs, and the "difference" I first saw was timing jitter in raw
  stderr. The check above forces overflow and compares token ids.)

PROTOCOL
--------
Randomised COMPLETE block: three rounds, each a random permutation of all eight
(arm, ctx) cells, so every arm appears exactly once per third of the session and
arm is orthogonal to block position BY CONSTRUCTION rather than by a lucky
shuffle. Position audit, mean slot: p0 11.67, p1 13.50, p1_noreencode 13.50,
p4 11.33 — balanced, as the design guarantees.

512-token prompt, 2048 decode, C in {1024, 2048}, -b/-ub 512, -fa off parsed
back as "disabled" on all 24 runs, greedy, seed 123, EOS disabled,
uninstrumented, 200 s cooldowns. All rc=0. p1, p1_noreencode and p4 decoded the
full 2048 at both budgets; p0 decoded 512 at C=1024 and 1536 at C=2048, which is
the halting policy behaving as designed. No exclusions, no flags.

THE PREDICTION, AND HOW IT LANDED
-----------------------------------
Predicted from the cooled b2 block at C=1024: p1 39.40, H2O 42.42, gap 3.02
(7.7%). If the attribution is right, the ablated arm should recover most of that
gap and land near H2O.

  measured gap at C=1024: 3.038 tok/s — the gap REPLICATES almost exactly
  (3.038 against the predicted 3.02), while both levels sit ~0.5 tok/s below the
  earlier block, a consistent block offset that cancels in the contrast.

  recovered by the ablation: +1.074 tok/s = 35.4% of the gap
  residual to H2O:           +1.964 tok/s = 64.6% of the gap

The ablated arm does NOT land near H2O. It lands about a third of the way there.

  C=1024:  p1 38.860  ->  ablated 39.935  ->  H2O 41.899      35.4% recovered
  C=2048:  p1 38.843  ->  ablated 39.538  ->  H2O 39.631      88.3% recovered

THE 35% / 88% SPLIT IS NOT A CHANGE IN THE MECHANISM'S IMPORTANCE
-------------------------------------------------------------------
It is tempting to read C=2048's 88.3% as the mechanism mattering more at larger
budgets. It does not. The re-encode's absolute cost FALLS between the two
budgets (1.074 -> 0.695 tok/s) while the denominator falls faster, because H2O
slows down and policy 1 does not:

    p1  38.860 -> 38.843   (flat; its cost is dominated by the shift, and the
                            shift happens on fewer steps at the larger C but
                            costs proportionally more per shift)
    H2O 41.899 -> 39.631   (slower: a 2048-cell cache is more attention work)

so the gap collapses from 3.038 to 0.787 and the same-sized effect fills more of
it. The percentage-of-gap statistic is a ratio of two quantities that scale
differently and should not be quoted as a property of the mechanism.

WHAT SCALES CORRECTLY, AND CONFIRMS THE MECHANISM'S IDENTITY
--------------------------------------------------------------
The per-shift cost is the quantity the "re-encodes the whole cache" story makes
a prediction about, and it is measured here for the first time. The number of
shifting steps is derived, not measured — the block is uninstrumented, and
UNIKV_LOG's shift_events column would have drained the pipeline. After the cache
fills, policy 1 evicts one cell per decode step, so every subsequent step shifts:

    C=1024: fills at generated token  512  ->  1536 of 2048 steps shift
    C=2048: fills at generated token 1536  ->   512 of 2048 steps shift

      C     shifting   p1 ms/tok  ablated  delta ms/tok  total ms  ms per shift
    1024        1536      25.733   25.041         0.692    1417.8         0.923
    2048         512      25.744   25.292         0.453     926.8         1.810

    per-shift cost ratio (C=2048 / C=1024) = 1.961
    predicted by "it re-encodes the whole cache" = 2.000

A 2x cache costs 1.96x per shift. The K-shift pass is linear in cache size, to
within 2%. That is a direct, quantitative confirmation that the operation
Finding 5 names is the operation being measured — and it is a stronger result
than the gap-fraction, because it tests the mechanism's SHAPE rather than its
share.

WHAT THE PAPER SHOULD SAY
--------------------------
Both halves, not just the first:

  "The rolling window pays a K-shift on every overflow, and that pass is linear
  in the resident cache: 0.92 ms per shift at C=1024 and 1.81 ms at C=2048, a
  ratio of 1.96 against the 2.00 that re-encoding the whole cache predicts.
  Ablating the pass recovers 1.07 tok/s of the 3.04 tok/s gap to H2O at C=1024
  (35%) and 0.70 of 0.79 at C=2048 (88%). The re-encode is therefore a
  confirmed mechanism but not the whole explanation at the smaller budget, where
  most of the gap remains unattributed."

The remaining 1.96 tok/s at C=1024 is NOT identified by this experiment and the
paper should not guess at it in print. Candidates that this block cannot
separate: seq_add's O(cache) scan on every shifting step; the slot search and
cell fragmentation that follow repeated renumbering; and the difference in how
the two policies leave the cache laid out for the next attention pass. Isolating
them needs another instrument, not another block.

LIMITS
------
- One model, one machine, one prompt length, two budgets, three trials.
- The shifting-step counts are derived from the eviction rule and the geometry,
  not measured, because the block is uninstrumented by protocol. The per-shift
  figures inherit that. The derivation is arithmetic (cache fills at C - prompt
  generated tokens, one eviction per step thereafter), but it is a derivation.
- The ablation removes the whole K-shift pass, so "0.92 ms per shift" is the
  cost of the pass including graph build and allocation, not of the rotary
  arithmetic alone. A finer instrument could split those.
- The ablated arm's output is invalid by construction and is never a quality
  result. Its throughput is meaningful only as the counterfactual "policy 1
  without this pass".
