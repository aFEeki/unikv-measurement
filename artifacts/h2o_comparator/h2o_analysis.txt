H2O fixed-budget eviction comparator (UNIKV_POLICY=4)
======================================================

Implementation: llama.cpp/src/llama-unikv-h2o.h, llama-graph.cpp (score

Traces: artifacts/quality_unified/logs/h2o_trace_h2o_p4_c1024.csv

HEADLINE
--------
H2O RETRIEVES THE PASSKEY. The paper's quality advantage holds against a naive
rolling window and against StreamingLLM; it does NOT hold against a competent
fixed-budget evictor on this probe. What survives is a narrower and different
claim: exactness, not retrieval.

WHAT WAS IMPLEMENTED
--------------------
H2O (Zhang et al., NeurIPS 2023): accumulate per-token attention mass across all
queries seen so far; at overflow keep the most recent `n_recent` tokens plus the
highest-mass tokens among the rest, evict the remainder. Budget split is the
paper's default, half recent and half heavy hitters (UNIKV_H2O_RECENT overrides).

In-fork, so it shares the binary, model (Llama-3.1-8B Q4_K_M), prompts, seed
(123), greedy decoding, batch tiling and thermal protocol with every other arm.
Attention probabilities are read from the explicit softmax, reduced INSIDE the
graph to one [1, n_kv] vector per layer (a transpose plus sum_rows), and folded
into a per-cell running total after each graph compute. Eviction removes cells
directly and does NOT call seq_add, so survivors keep their absolute positions:
RoPE stays exact and the position-gap failure that affected policy 1 cannot
arise. Survivors are non-contiguous in both cell index and position, which
find_slot handles because it tests cells one at a time when cont=false.

TWO DEVIATIONS FROM THE PAPER, BOTH FORCED, BOTH DISADVANTAGING H2O
--------------------------------------------------------------------
1. H2O is defined PER HEAD. A llama.cpp KV cell is shared by every layer and
   head, so evicting a cell removes that token everywhere; importance must be
   aggregated over heads and layers into a single score per token. Any
   implementation on a shared-cell cache must do this. It makes the policy
   strictly LESS selective than per-head H2O, so it understates H2O if anything.
2. Accumulated mass is a running sum, as the paper specifies, not an average, so
   newly admitted tokens start at zero and are protected only by the recent
   window — also as in the paper.

Neither deviation was chosen to weaken the comparator. Both are noted because a
reviewer will ask, and because the result went in H2O's favour anyway.

CORRECTNESS EVIDENCE (this is not a strawman)
----------------------------------------------
1. INERT WHEN IDLE. At C=4096 with a 512-token prompt (no overflow), policy 4
   emits a token-ID-identical sequence to stock policy 0. The score-extraction
   machinery does not perturb the computation.
2. BEATS THE NAIVE WINDOW AT THE SAME BUDGET — the pre-registered sanity check.
   At C=1024 on the passkey probe, the naive window loses the passkey and H2O
   retrieves it.
3. THE RANKING IS SEMANTICALLY CORRECT. From the survivor trace (1023 resident
   cells, budget C=1024):
       pos     0   score 2245263.2   <- attention sink, 150x the next token
       pos   199   score   14346.1   <- passkey
       pos    30   score   12322.6
       pos   218   score   11965.8   <- passkey
       pos   207   score    6277.4
       pos    44   score    5519.0
   The passkey tokens are the #2 and #4 heavy hitters in the entire cache, and
   the position-0 sink dominates by two orders of magnitude — exactly the
   behaviour the H2O and StreamingLLM literature describes. A misread signal
   could not produce that ordering.
4. THE BUDGET IS REAL. 1023 of 1024 cells resident, 512 in the recent window and
   511 heavy hitters, 231 survivors drawn from the first 512 prompt positions.
   H2O is genuinely retaining old high-attention tokens, not approximating a
   window.
5. Two cells of 1023 carry small negative accumulated sums (min -6.4 against a
   median of 233) — float noise in the in-graph reduction, immaterial to any
   ranking, recorded rather than swept up.

So the ordering is: naive breaks the mechanism, StreamingLLM fixes the mechanism
but loses the information, H2O keeps the information but not the computation, and
UniKV keeps both.

WHAT THE PAPER HAS TO CHANGE
-----------------------------
The current framing — UniKV retrieves where eviction loses — is true only against
window-based eviction. Against H2O it is false. The defensible claim is:

  Fixed-budget eviction can retain a specific fact if that fact attracts
  attention while it is still resident. What it cannot do is reproduce the
  computation that would have happened without eviction. UniKV's output is
  token-identical to the no-eviction reference; H2O's is not, despite recovering
  the same fact.

That is a claim about EXACTNESS, which is what Proposition 1 is actually about
(lossless retention), and it is the honest form of the contribution. It also
means the passkey probe no longer separates UniKV from good prior art — only the
token-identity check does. Review item D6 already observed that the probe is
guaranteed for a lossless policy by construction; this result shows it is also
achievable for a lossy one, which removes it as a discriminator entirely.

H2O IS THE FASTEST CONTINUATION POLICY AT BOTH BUDGETS
-------------------------------------------------------
  C=1024:  UniKV is 29.8% slower than H2O (29.05 vs 41.41)
  C=2048:  UniKV is 11.6% slower than H2O (34.64 vs 39.19)

H2O also beats the naive rolling window (+7.5% at C=1024, +1.9% at C=2048),
which is not obvious and is worth one sentence in the paper: policy 1 calls
seq_add on every overflow, re-encoding RoPE across the whole cache, while H2O
drops cells outright and touches no positions. The "cheap" baseline is the more
expensive mechanism.

So the exactness premium — what the paper is really selling — is about 30% of
throughput at C=1024 against a comparator that ALSO retrieves the passkey, on a
device where the theory says the transfer term is zero. That is the number the
paper should quote, in place of any comparison against the halting baseline.

A REVIEWER WILL ASK WHY H2O SUCCEEDS HERE
------------------------------------------
Because the needle attracts attention during prefill. The passkey is a rare,
high-information token in repetitive filler, so it accumulates mass well above
the filler median long before the closing question is seen. H2O's known failure
mode — it must decide what matters before it sees the query — is not exercised by
this prompt. A probe designed to defeat accumulated-attention selection (a needle
that is NOT attended until the question arrives) would likely separate them. That
would be a fair experiment to run; it would also be transparently a probe chosen
to make the comparator fail, and should be presented as a targeted stress test
rather than as the headline if it is run at all.

LIMITS
------
- One probe, one needle position, one budget split (512/512). The heavy/recent
  split is H2O's default but is a free parameter; it was not swept.
- Aggregating importance across heads and layers is a real departure from the
  paper (see deviation 1). A per-head implementation would need a per-head cache
  layout, which llama.cpp does not have.
- SnapKV, the other candidate, was not implemented. It compresses once at the end
  of prefill using the last window of queries; on this probe it would face the
  same question H2O did and the same answer would likely apply.
- No cross-stack comparator (vLLM CPU swap, FastDecode-class), per the explicit
  scope decision: a different tokenizer and different kernels would confound the
  comparison. The taxonomy in Section 2 still contains twelve unevaluated systems.
