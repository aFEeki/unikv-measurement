DECISION: DRAIN-CONTROL BLOCK ALONE. NOT SPLICED WITH THE COOLED SWEEP.
------------------------------------------------------------------------
The overlay was genuinely on the table. The two blocks share an alpha=0
undrained point and agree to 0.30% (sweep 29.673, drain control 29.762), and
they also agree to 0.41% at alpha=0.25 drained (29.187 vs 29.068). On level,
they are interchangeable.

They are NOT interchangeable on slope, which is what this figure claims:

    drained slope, drain-control block   -1.991 +/- 0.300 tok/s per unit alpha
                                         (2 points, alpha 0 -> 0.25)
    drained slope, cooled sweep          -1.197 +/- 0.052 tok/s per unit alpha
                                         (n=18 fit, alpha 0.25 -> 2)
    difference                           -0.794, t = -2.61

Two points settle it:

1. A shared level anchor calibrates LEVEL. This figure's entire claim is that
   two series differ in SLOPE, and the one cross-block slope comparison
   available disagrees at t = 2.6. Splicing would assert exactly the property
   the data declines to support.

2. The spliced drained series would carry a visible kink at alpha=0.25 — steep
   from the drain-control segment, shallower from the sweep — that no reader
   could attribute to curvature rather than to the block change. Producing an
   unattributable feature is worse than producing a shorter series.

(Whether that -1.99 vs -1.20 difference is real curvature in the first quarter
of alpha or a block effect is not resolvable from these data. It is a fair thing
for a future block to settle, by running drained points beyond alpha=0.25 in the
same block as the undrained ones.)

The within-block statistic is in any case the stronger one. At alpha=0.25, the
only alpha where both conditions were measured:

    undrained 29.781 - drained 29.068 = +0.713 +/- 0.068 tok/s,  t = 10.5

That single contrast carries the finding, and it needs no second block.

WHAT THE FIGURE DRAWS
---------------------

             alpha 0.25  29.781 +/- 0.110
             alpha 1.00  29.221 +/- 0.145
  drained    alpha 0.00  29.566 +/- 0.123
             alpha 0.25  29.068 +/- 0.042

Error bars are the per-condition SDs, as specified. The vertical arrow at
alpha=0.25 marks the 0.713 tok/s gap with its t statistic. Slopes are given in
the legend rather than beside the lines, so "-1.99" cannot be misread as a data
value. A note marks that the drained series stops at alpha=0.25 because that is
where the block's data stops — the series is not extrapolated.

ONE CORRECTION TO HOW THIS RESULT HAS BEEN DESCRIBED
-----------------------------------------------------
The undrained series is NOT flat, and the figure does not draw it as flat.

    undrained, alpha 0 -> 0.25:  +0.019 +/- 0.174  (t =  0.11)  invisible
    undrained, alpha 0 -> 1:     -0.541 +/- 0.183  (t = -2.96)  real

Over the full range the undrained arm loses 1.8% of throughput, and that decline
is statistically real. What the block shows is that it is much SHALLOWER than
the drained arm: -0.588 against -1.991 tok/s per unit alpha, a factor of 3.4.

So the claim the figure supports is "an injected delay is largely absorbed
unless the pipeline is drained", not "an injected delay is invisible unless the
pipeline is drained". At alpha=0.25 the delay is genuinely invisible (t = 0.11);
by alpha=1, with the sleep at roughly 3.1 ms against a ~34 ms step, absorption
is no longer complete. That is the physically expected behaviour — overlap
degrades as the injected delay approaches the step time — and it is worth one
clause in the caption rather than being smoothed over.

AXIS
----
y is truncated to 28.85-30.28 and the truncation is marked with a break glyph on
the axis. Justification, per the standing convention that fig5 keeps a zero
baseline: fig5's caption quotes percentage reductions, so a truncated axis there
would visually overstate them. This figure claims a difference in SLOPE, not the
size of a ratio, and the entire effect spans 0.7 tok/s out of ~29.5 — on a zero
baseline it would be invisible. Truncation is the right call here and the wrong
call there.
