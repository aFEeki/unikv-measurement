Review item M2 — exact retention is NOT bit-exact. DA-1 was right.
================================================================================
Run 2026-08-21. Harnesses: scripts/run_logit_bound.py (main),
                           scripts/run_logit_controls.py (controls)
Data:    quality_results/logit_bound.csv, raw float32 dumps in
         artifacts/logit_bound/*.bin
Console: artifacts/logit_bound/run.log
Code:    UNIKV_LOGIT_LOG in tools/completion/completion.cpp — dumps the RAW model
         logits for every sampled step, before the sampler touches them.

HEADLINE
--------
Over the 512-token horizon, exact retention reproduces the no-eviction
reference's TOKEN stream perfectly (512/512, both prompts, both tiers) while its
LOGITS differ by up to 2.18. Not one step out of 512 is bit-identical on either
tier. The recommendation the paper makes is empirically true and structurally
unguaranteed, and the difference matters.

  max |delta logit| vs the C=8192 no-eviction reference
                          passkey     prose     steps bit-identical   tokens
    p3_dev  (C=1024)       0.1726    0.2806            0/512         512/512
    p3_cpu  (C=1024)       1.9920    2.1843            0/512         512/512
    p4_h2o  (C=1024)      33.4892   45.1159            0/512    30/512, 164/512

THE CONFOUND, RULED OUT BEFORE ANYTHING WAS CONCLUDED
------------------------------------------------------
The reference runs at C=8192 and the retention arms at C=1024. Those differ in
two ways at once: the spill mechanism, and the size of the attention window,
which changes the shape of the reduction and hence float summation order.
Non-associativity alone could have produced the whole effect. Two controls:

  A. DETERMINISM. policy 0, C=8192, run twice with identical settings:
     max |delta| = 0 over 64 steps, 64/64 bit-identical. The runtime is
     deterministic, so every delta below is signal.

  B. GRAPH SHAPE. policy 0 at C=8192 vs policy 0 at C=4096, NO SPILL ANYWHERE,
     compared over the 262 generated tokens before C=4096 fills:
     max |delta| = 0, 262/262 bit-identical.

     Cache size alone does NOT move the logits on this runtime. The floor is
     exactly zero, so the deltas above are attributable to the spill-and-recall
     path and not to reduction-order differences. This was the result that could
     have invalidated the whole measurement, and it did not.

  C. TIER. policy 3 CPU-pinned vs policy 3 device-visible, both C=1024, same
     spilled set: max |delta| = 2.035. The two tiers differ from EACH OTHER by
     about as much as the CPU-pinned tier differs from the reference.

MECHANISM
---------
The ordering is consistent and has an obvious cause. The device-visible tier
keeps the recall on Metal and lands 0.17-0.28 from the reference. The CPU-pinned
tier is 11.5x further out at 1.99-2.18, and it is the arm that computes part of
attention on the CPU — the two pins in build_attn_mha_unikv that policy 3
requires when the spilled tier is host-resident. A different backend for part of
the reduction is a different summation order and a different intermediate
precision, so its arithmetic cannot match an all-Metal reference.

"Exact" was always a claim about the DATA — spilled K/V are recalled, not
approximated, and that remains true. It was never a claim about the arithmetic,
and the paper has been reading it as though it were.

HOW CLOSE DID TOKEN IDENTITY COME TO BREAKING? — SPLIT BY TIER
----------------------------------------------------------------
Pooling the two tiers could not answer this, and the split changes the
conclusion. Per step, m is the REFERENCE's top1-top2 margin and eps is the arm's
max |delta logit| at that step. m <= 2*eps is the conservative flip condition: a
perturbation of size eps applied adversarially to both the winner and the
runner-up closes a margin of 2*eps.

  prompt   arm         max eps   min(m - 2 eps)   steps m<=2eps   actual flips
  passkey  p3_cpu       1.9920      -0.04647            6              0
  passkey  p3_dev       0.1726      -0.03500            1              0
  prose    p3_cpu       2.1843      -0.02535            2              0
  prose    p3_dev       0.2806      -0.00711            1              0

  reference top1-top2 margin: passkey min 0.00879 (median 9.52)
                              prose   min 0.00108 (median 7.23)

DEVICE-VISIBLE IS NOT THE SAFER TIER. It is the less-perturbed tier, and those
are different claims. Its worst-case slack is -0.0350 against the CPU-pinned
tier's -0.0465 on the passkey prompt — the same order of magnitude — even though
its perturbation is 11.5x smaller. It has fewer at-risk steps (1 against 6 and 2),
which is a real but modest advantage, and on the prose prompt the counts are 1
against 2.

The reason is that the at-risk steps are not selected by perturbation size. They
are the steps where the REFERENCE is itself nearly tied: the minimum reference
margin is 0.00879 and 0.00108, against medians of 9.52 and 7.23. When the model
is that close to indifferent between two tokens, a perturbation of almost any
size puts the step at risk, and the tier's advantage in magnitude buys very
little. The binding constraint is the reference's own margin distribution, not
the tier.

So the honest move is to DOWNGRADE BOTH TIERS rather than recommend one. Neither
is bit-exact, both have steps where a flip is not excluded by the arithmetic, and
the device-visible tier's advantage is in how often that happens rather than in
how close the worst case comes.

heavy-tailed and the mean and the median tell different stories:

THE CORRECTED READING. What grows on the CPU-pinned tier is the TAIL, not the
body. Its median eps rises only 1.44x across the horizon on passkey and 1.35x on
prose, while its max/median ratio rises 8.1x -> 59.7x on passkey. The 2.94x
"growth" reported from the means is that tail pulling the mean, not the typical
step getting worse. On prose the tail ratio has no trend at all, so even the tail
growth is one prompt out of two.

THE DEVICE-VISIBLE TIER IS FLAT IN BOTH. Median and mean are flat or slightly
falling on both prompts, and its max/median ratio has no trend. That remains the
signature of a fixed reassociation difference that does not accumulate.

eps IS HEAVY-TAILED ON THE CPU-PINNED ARM, which is worth stating on its own:
overall max/median is 81x (passkey) and 129x (prose) against the device-visible
tier's 20x and 62x. So on that arm most steps are perturbed by ~0.02 and rare
steps by 1-2 logits.

The paper already documents that same arm as heavy-tailed in TIME ("host-side
attention does not merely cost more per step, it costs more variably"). It is now
also heavy-tailed in ARITHMETIC. WHETHER THE TWO TAILS SHARE A MECHANISM IS NOT
SETTLED BY THIS DATA AND SHOULD NOT BE CLAIMED — they are two observations about
the same arm, measured on different quantities, and the co-occurrence is worth
one sentence and no inference.

WHAT THIS DOES TO THE PAPER
-----------------------------
DA-1's objection is VALIDATED with a number. The instrument the paper used —
token identity under greedy argmax — cannot distinguish exact from near-exact,
and the arms it certified as identical are near-exact with a measurable gap.
Section 9's recommendation should be restated in the form the evidence supports:

  "Exact retention reproduced the no-eviction reference's token stream for 512
  generated tokens on two prompts, two spill tiers and two model architectures.
  It is not bit-exact: logits differ from the reference by up to 0.28
  (device-visible) and 2.18 (CPU-pinned), and no step is bit-identical. On both
  tiers there are steps where the reference's own top1-top2 margin is smaller
  than twice the perturbation, so a flip is not excluded by the arithmetic — one
  such step on the device-visible tier and two to six on the CPU-pinned tier,
  out of 512, with none actually flipping. Reproducible decode is an empirical
  property of these runs, not a guarantee of the mechanism."

Two things follow, and the first is a correction to what this file said before
the tiers were separated:

  1. DO NOT RECOMMEND THE DEVICE-VISIBLE TIER ON SAFETY GROUNDS. It is perturbed
     11.5x less, but its worst-case slack (-0.0350) is the same order as the
     CPU-pinned tier's (-0.0465), because the at-risk steps are selected by the
     REFERENCE being nearly tied rather than by the perturbation's size. The
     supportable statement is that it has fewer at-risk steps (1 against 2-6),
     not that it is safe where the other is not. Both tiers should be downgraded
     from "exact" to "token-identical on these runs, not bit-exact".
  2. THE CPU-PINNED TIER'S CLAIM IS STILL THE WEAKEST IN THE PAPER. It is the
     slowest arm, the heavy-tailed arm (Section 4's step-time tail), the
     arithmetically loosest, and the only one whose perturbation may grow with
     horizon. Bounding device memory is a real property and should be the only
     thing claimed for it.

LIMITS
------
- Two prompts, one model, one budget, 512 tokens. Deterministic, so single runs
  are sufficient for the arithmetic; they are not sufficient for a claim about
  the distribution of margins across prompts.
- max |delta| is over the whole 128256-token vocabulary; the flip analysis is the
  operative quantity and is reported separately for that reason.
- The controls establish that cache size alone contributes exactly zero ON THIS
  RUNTIME with these settings. That is a stronger result than expected and should
  not be assumed to hold with flash attention on, or on another backend.
- No claim is made about whether a flip WOULD occur on some other prompt. The
  measurement bounds the perturbation and the observed margins; it does not
  bound the margin distribution.
