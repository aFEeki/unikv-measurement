steps (artifacts/qwen_cooled/logs/step_*):

The device-visible tier is essentially deterministic on BOTH models — p99 within

trials, now reproduced on a second model, at a different spilled-set size, on a
quiet machine. It is a property of the CPU-pinned tier, not a machine artifact.

CONSEQUENCE FOR THE FIT: the CPU-pinned mean-based slope is inflated and
imprecise (SE 0.4135 against device-visible's 0.0058; residual SD 4.473 ms
against 0.063). Every CPU-pinned claim below is therefore reported on BOTH
estimators, and no CPU-pinned conclusion is drawn that the two disagree about.
They agree on everything that matters here.

PREDICTION 1 — delta: bandwidth to the pages, or per-cell overhead?
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Predicted: 0.4375 x Llama's if delta is a BYTES cost; 1.0 x if it is a per-cell
overhead independent of size.

  CPU-pinned      [means]   llama 3.0990  qwen 3.5779 +/- 0.4135   ratio 1.155
                    vs bytes (1.3558): t = +5.37  REJECTED
                    vs unchanged      : t = +1.14  consistent
                  [medians] llama 2.8531  qwen 2.6667 +/- 0.2136   ratio 0.935
                    vs bytes (1.2483): t = +6.64  REJECTED
                    vs unchanged      : t = -0.85  consistent

  device-visible  [means]   llama 2.1531  qwen 1.7174 +/- 0.0058   ratio 0.798
                    vs bytes (0.9420): t = +132.6  REJECTED
                    vs unchanged      : t =  -54.5  REJECTED
                  [medians] llama 2.1549  qwen 1.7184 +/- 0.0062   ratio 0.797
                    (both estimators agree to the third decimal)

ANSWER: the two tiers give different answers, and that is the result.

  On the CPU-PINNED tier delta does NOT track bytes. It is consistent with being
  unchanged across a 2.3x change in KV per cell, on both estimators. Whatever
  delta measures there, it is charged per CELL, not per byte.

  On the DEVICE-VISIBLE tier delta rejects BOTH pure hypotheses. It sits at
  0.798 +/- 0.003 of Llama's, strictly between 0.4375 and 1.0, and the two
  estimators agree to three decimals. Solving delta = A + B x (KiB per cell)
  across the two blocks:

      A = 1.379 us/cell (size-independent)   B = 6.05 ns/cell per KiB

  so at Llama's 128 KiB/cell the bytes term is 36.0% of delta and the fixed
  per-cell term is 64.0%; at Qwen's 56 KiB/cell the bytes term is 19.7%.

This is the separation Finding 1 says it cannot make, and the honest form of the
answer is "both mechanisms, in a proportion that depends on the tier" rather
than either single mechanism.

  CAVEAT ON THE DECOMPOSITION, WHICH MATTERS: with exactly two models, A and B
  are determined by two equations in two unknowns. There is no residual and no
  degrees of freedom — that is a DESCRIPTION consistent with both blocks, not a
  fitted law, and it cannot be checked without a third model. What is genuinely
  established is the REJECTION of both one-parameter predictions on the
  device-visible arm, which is estimator-stable and enormous in t.

  CONFOUND, ALSO IMPORTANT: the two models do not differ only in bytes per cell.
  KV/cell factors as n_layer x per-layer width: 0.4375 = (28/32) x (512/1024)
  = 0.875 x 0.5. A purely PER-LAYER cost would predict a ratio of 0.875. The
  measured device-visible ratio is 0.798 +/- 0.003, which rejects 0.875 too
  (t = 23) — but it lies much closer to the per-layer prediction than to the
  bytes prediction. Two models cannot separate a per-layer term from a per-byte
  term, because both differ together. A third model with Llama's layer count and
  Qwen's KV width (or the reverse) would separate them, and that is the specific
  experiment this result now motivates.

PREDICTION 2 — gamma: is the fixed term model-independent? NO. THIS FAILS.
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Predicted roughly model-independent, near 8.8 ms CPU-pinned and 2.7 ms
device-visible, on the argument that gamma is a graph-shape and handoff cost.

  CPU-pinned      [means]   llama 8.762 ms  qwen 4.119 ms   ratio 0.470
                  [medians] llama 8.640 ms  qwen 5.216 ms   ratio 0.604
  device-visible  [means]   llama 2.708 ms  qwen 1.918 ms   ratio 0.708
                  [medians] llama 2.656 ms  qwen 1.906 ms   ratio 0.718

gamma is NOT model-independent. It falls by 30% on the device-visible tier and
by 40-53% on the CPU-pinned tier. The paper attributes gamma to graph shape and
tier handoff; on that argument a 28-layer model with half the per-layer KV width
should have paid roughly the same fixed entry cost, and it did not.

The direction is informative rather than merely negative. On the CPU-pinned tier
the gamma ratio (0.470 on means) is close to the KV-bytes ratio (0.4375), and on
the device-visible tier it is closer to the layer ratio (0.875) than to bytes
but below both. So gamma carries model-dependent work — plausibly a per-layer or
per-byte staging cost paid once on entry to the two-tier path — that the paper
currently books as a fixed property of the graph. The paper should either
measure what gamma is made of or stop claiming it is model-independent.

Note the estimator disagreement on CPU-pinned gamma (0.470 vs 0.604) is larger
than elsewhere, because gamma inherits the heavy-tailed intercept. The claim
"gamma is not model-independent" survives both; the ratio should be quoted as a
range, not a point.

PREDICTION 3 — the device-visible reduction
---------------------------------------------
The paper claims device-visibility removes 69% of gamma and 24.5-30.5% of delta.

              gamma reduction   delta (means)   delta (medians)
  llama            69.1%            30.5%           24.5%
  qwen             53.5%            52.0%           35.6%

Neither figure carries over. The gamma reduction is smaller on Qwen (53.5% vs
69.1%) and the delta reduction is LARGER (52.0% vs 30.5% on means; 35.6% vs
24.5% on medians). The qualitative claim — device-visibility reduces both terms,
and the two slopes are separable — replicates on both models. The percentages do
not, and should be presented as this-model numbers rather than as properties of
the technique.

WHAT REPLICATED, PLAINLY
-------------------------
  - policies 3 and 4 construct and run on a second architecture
  - the no-spill references of the two tier modes agree (t = -0.66 here,
    matching the Llama check) — the tier is inert at idle
  - the affine form holds on both tiers on both models
  - device-visible delta < CPU-pinned delta, separable (t = 4.50 here, 12.34 there)
  - the CPU-pinned tier's heavy-tailed step time, at a different spilled-set size
  - device-visible step times are near-deterministic (p99 within 1% of median)

WHAT DID NOT
-------------
  - gamma is not model-independent (prediction 2 fails outright)
  - the 69% / 24.5-30.5% device-visible reductions are model-specific
  - delta is not a single mechanism: per-cell on the CPU-pinned tier, mixed on
    the device-visible tier

LIMITS
------
- Two models, one machine, one runtime, one quantization (Q4_K_M), one C, one
  burst length. The prediction test is only as strong as two points allow.
- The layer/width confound above is the binding limitation on the delta result
  and is not resolvable from this pair.
- The CPU-pinned coefficients here are imprecise by comparison with every other
  cell in either block; treat them as a range.
- These two blocks are never pooled. Every cross-model number above is a
  comparison of two independently fitted blocks, which is what makes it a
  prediction test rather than a merged dataset.
