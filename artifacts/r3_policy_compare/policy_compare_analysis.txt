QUESTION
--------
Two things at once:
 (a) M6 — the most-cited throughput numbers in the paper (42.5 / 33.0 / 28.5)
     come from a single uncooled comparison run, on a device the paper itself
     documents swinging 21% from heat. Do they survive the cooled
     randomized-block protocol used for the alpha sweep?
 (b) M1 / DA-3 — the paper compares UniKV only against policy 0, which errors
     out at cache-full. Policy 1 (the rolling-window shift, the mechanism
     upstream llama.cpp ships as --context-shift) also completes the budget and
     is the comparator a practitioner would actually reach for. What does the
     comparison look like when it is included?

PROTOCOL
--------
512-token prompt + 2048-token decode budget; -fa off, -fit off, greedy
(temp 0), seed 123, --ignore-eos, -b 512 -ub 512, UNIKV_ALPHA=0.
6 arms x 3 trials = 18 runs, randomized block (shuffle seed 20260803), 200 s
cooldown before every run — the same protocol as the cooled alpha sweep.

Runs are UNINSTRUMENTED (no UNIKV_LOG). That matters: when UNIKV_LOG is set the
library calls synchronize() at both ends of every decode call, so the per-step
logging itself drains the pipeline every step. The superseded Run-1 numbers were
instrumented; these are not, which also makes them directly comparable to the
cooled alpha sweep's alpha=0 anchor (29.67 tok/s).

Every row's flash_attn setting was parsed back out of that run's own log rather
than trusted from the harness source: all 18 rows report flash_attn = disabled.
All rc = 0. All arms under the 58 tok/s ceiling.

THE COMPARATOR PROBLEM (M1 / DA-3), MEASURED
--------------------------------------------
At C=1024, against the error-out baseline:  2048 vs 512 tokens = the "4x".
At C=1024, against the rolling-window shift: 2048 vs 2048 tokens = 1x.

So the token-count advantage is entirely an advantage over a configuration that
refuses to continue, not over the continuation policy the same codebase ships.
The difference against policy 1 is not how much is generated but what: policy 1
completes the budget by discarding, policy 3 by retaining (see the unified
quality harness for the retrieval consequence).

The throughput price of losslessness, measured at fixed C and fixed token count:
  C=1024:  38.64 -> 28.90 tok/s  = 25.2% slower than the lossy comparator
  C=2048:  38.64 -> 34.98 tok/s  =  9.5% slower

A NUMBER THE PAPER SHOULD STOP USING THIS WAY
---------------------------------------------
"42.5 -> 28.5 is pure recall cost" does not survive this block. 42.5 is the
policy-0 arm, and that arm HALTS after 512 decode tokens: its rate is measured
over a short decode phase whose KV occupancy only ever reaches C, and it never
pays for continuing past cache-full at all. Comparing it to a 2048-token
policy-3 run charges the recall mechanism for the cost of continuing to exist.
(PROJECT_STATE already retires the baseline as a rate comparator for exactly
this reason under DEAD NUMBERS; the 42.5 -> 28.5 framing reintroduced it.)

The like-for-like recall cost — same C, same device KV, same token count, same
flash-attention setting, same block — is 38.64 -> 28.90 at C=1024 (25.2%) and
38.64 -> 34.98 at C=2048 (9.5%), i.e. it scales with the spilled set (1535 vs
511 cells), which is the claim the cost model actually needs.

SIDE OBSERVATION (physically sensible, worth one line in the paper)
-------------------------------------------------------------------
p1_c1024 and p1_c2048 are identical to three digits (38.64 +/- 0.03 both), and
p0 differs only 42.59 vs 40.74 across a 2x context change. Decode is dominated
by streaming the ~4.6 GiB of Q4_K_M weights per token; the KV read is 128-256
MiB, a few percent of that. Context size is a second-order term for the
GPU-resident policies — which is precisely why the CPU-pinned spilled tier
(a per-step cost that is NOT weight-bandwidth-bound) shows up so clearly.

LIMITS
------
- n=3 per arm. SDs are small but these are three trials on one machine on one
  night, not a distributional claim.
- Uninstrumented, so no per-step shift counts for policy 1 in this block; the
  spill counts quoted for policy 3 come from its stderr INFO lines. A separate
  instrumented pass (UNIKV_PC_STEPLOG=1) is needed if per-call shift counts for
  policy 1 are wanted for the paper.
- One p3_c1024 trial (order 17, 29.38) sits ~0.7 tok/s above the other two
  (28.64, 28.69) and is what makes that arm's sd 0.41 rather than ~0.03. It is
  the last p3_c1024 in the block and the fastest, so it is not a heat-soak
  artifact; unexplained, and left in rather than trimmed.
