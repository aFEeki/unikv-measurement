ITEM 1 — DOES THE EPISODE THREATEN THE HEADLINE MARGIN? NO.
-------------------------------------------------------------
The episode: slots 19-24 of the M1 block are all depressed (mean -0.261 tok/s,
worst -0.531) while slots 1-18 are all at or above their cell means (+0.087).
It is a STEP at slot 19, not a ramp.

  p1_amortized - p4_h2o, C=1024
    DRIFT      -0.0272 +/- 0.0067 tok/s per slot (t = -4.09, real)
    RAW        +0.667 +/- 0.099 tok/s
    DETRENDED  +0.667          (shift +0.000)
    LOCAL      +0.677 +/- 0.025 (shift +0.010)
  p1_amortized - p4_h2o, C=2048
    DRIFT      -0.0077 +/- 0.0034 tok/s per slot (t = -2.27)
    RAW        +0.711 +/- 0.056 tok/s
    DETRENDED  +0.742          (shift +0.031)
    LOCAL      +0.728 +/- 0.026 (shift +0.017)

All three agree with raw within its uncertainty and the sign is never at risk, so
by the stated decision rule THE FINDING STANDS AS MEASURED and no re-run is
needed.

WHY the drift cancels: the randomised COMPLETE block gave p1_amortized a mean
slot of 12.00 and p4_h2o a mean slot of 12.00 — identical. Detrending therefore
shifts the C=1024 contrast by exactly 0.000. This is the complete-block design
doing the job the drain block's plain shuffle failed to do; it is worth noting
that the protection was structural rather than lucky.

A FOURTH CHECK, BECAUSE THE THREE ASSUME MONOTONE DRIFT AND THIS IS A STEP
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
Detrending and local interpolation both model a ramp. A step at slot 19 is not a
ramp, and local interpolation across it actively over-corrects: it propagates
slot 22's depression backwards to slot 19. So the episode was also handled the
way a step should be — by excluding it.

  C=1024, pre-episode (slots < 19) vs all slots
    p1_amortized  42.513 vs 42.433   (+0.079)
    p1_window     38.795 vs 38.530   (+0.265)
    p4_h2o        41.824 vs 41.766   (+0.057)

  VALIDATION: pre-episode p1_window is 38.795 against the independent six-trial
  recheck's 38.792 — a difference of 0.009%. The exclusion reproduces a
  separately measured value on a different day, which is the strongest evidence
  available that it removes the episode rather than trimming to taste.

CONSEQUENCE FOR THE TWO CLAIMS
--------------------------------
  amortized over the per-token window, C=1024:  +10.13% raw -> +9.58% corrected
  amortized over the per-token window, C=2048:   +3.80% raw ->  +3.94% corrected
  amortized over H2O,                 C=1024:   +1.60% raw ->  +1.65% corrected
  amortized over H2O,                 C=2048:   +1.80% raw ->  +1.94% corrected

THE +10.13% WAS AN OVERESTIMATE, as suspected, because the episode hit the
p1_window arm's slot-22 run (-0.80 from its own cell) far harder than it hit the
p1_amortized arm's slot-19 run (-0.24). The corrected figure is +9.58%.

The H2O margin is robust and if anything slightly larger after correction:
+0.667 raw, +0.677 local, +0.689 pre-episode. Four estimators, same sign, spread
of 0.022 tok/s.

SO THE TWO BLOCKS DISAGREE ON THE RATIO — BUT NOT SIGNIFICANTLY
-----------------------------------------------------------------
  per-event, 95% intervals            C=1024                C=2048
    published block            0.924 [0.902, 0.945]   1.814 [1.710, 1.917]
    recheck, n=6, new binary   0.937 [0.914, 0.959]   1.722 [1.670, 1.775]

    ratio, published block     1.964  95% CI [1.844, 2.085]  contains 2.000: YES
    ratio, recheck             1.839  95% CI [1.768, 1.911]  contains 2.000: NO
    difference of the ratios   +0.125 95% CI [-0.015, +0.266] -> CONTAINS ZERO

  recovery, compared directly:
    C=1024  published +1.074 +/- 0.012  recheck +1.086 +/- 0.013  t = -0.66
    C=2048  published +0.695 +/- 0.020  recheck +0.660 +/- 0.010  t = +1.53

The two blocks AGREE on recovery at both budgets and their ratios are not
significantly different. What has changed is not the measurement but its apparent
precision: the published block's ratio of 1.964 looked like clean support for
2.000, and its own 95% interval is [1.844, 2.085] — wide enough that it never
excluded 1.85 either. The recheck's tighter interval excludes 2.000, but only
just, at an upper bound of 1.911.

THE HONEST CONCLUSION IS NOT "THE LINEARITY RESULT FAILED TO REPLICATE". It is
that neither block has the precision to settle linearity, and Section 8 presents
a point estimate as though it did. The supportable statement is that the
per-event cost roughly doubles when the resident cache doubles — measured at
1.84 and 1.96 in two blocks whose intervals overlap and straddle 2.000 — not
that it is 1.961 against a predicted 2.000.

RECONSTRUCTION VERDICT UNDER THE INTERVAL RULE — READ IT BOTH WAYS
--------------------------------------------------------------------
The rule as stated: pass if the block's 95% interval on per-event cost contains
the published value at both budgets. C=1024 contains 0.923 (pass). C=2048's
interval [1.670, 1.775] does NOT contain the published 1.814, with tight bars.
BY THE LETTER OF THE RULE, THIS FAILS.

The symmetric reading disagrees, and the difference is not cosmetic: the rule
compares a new interval against an OLD POINT, treating 1.814 as exact when it is
itself an estimate with interval [1.710, 1.917]. The two intervals overlap over
[1.710, 1.775], the difference of the two ratios contains zero, and the recovery
statistics agree at t = -0.66 and t = +1.53.

The reconstruction is therefore consistent with the original instrument on every
symmetric test, and fails only a test that gives the original block's estimate
zero uncertainty. That judgement is the author's to make, which is why the commit
is being held rather than taken.

LIMITS
------
- The episode's cause is not identified. The unified log is not queryable in this
  environment, so machine idleness during the M1 block cannot be verified. What
  is established is that it is confined to slots 19-24, that it is a step, and
  that excluding it reproduces an independently measured value to 0.009%.
- The pre-episode means at C=1024 rest on n=2 per arm. They are corroborated by
  the six-trial recheck for p1_window only; p1_amortized and p4_h2o have no
  independent re-measurement.
- Ratios of per-event costs amplify the uncertainty of two small differences of
  large numbers. That is why both blocks' intervals are wide despite tight
  per-arm standard errors, and it is the reason the linearity claim cannot be
  settled at this sample size rather than a defect of either block.
