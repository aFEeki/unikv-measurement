Is the drain cost alpha-independent? No. The 71/29 split does not survive.
================================================================================
Run 2026-08-11. Harness: scripts/run_drain_alpha1.py
Data:  alpha_results/p3_drain_alpha1_master.csv (12 runs)
Logs:  artifacts/drain_alpha1/logs/, console artifacts/drain_alpha1/run.log
Supersedes the DECOMPOSITION section of
artifacts/drain_control/drain_control_analysis.txt. Does NOT affect fig7, whose
plotted values and within-block gap are unchanged.

QUESTION
--------
The published decomposition splits the alpha=0 -> alpha=0.25 step into 29% drain
and 71% modeled transfer. That split assumes the pipeline-drain cost is the same
at every alpha. The first drain-control block never tested it: it measured drain
cost only at alpha=0 and its one alpha=1 point was undrained. This block closes
the 2x2.

DESIGN
------
Four cells, 3 trials each, one block, randomized (seed 20260811), 200 s cooldown
before every run including the first, uninstrumented (UNIKV_LOG unset — it
synchronizes at both ends of every decode call and would drain the arms whose
whole point is that they do not). Otherwise identical to the first block: policy
3, C=1024, 512-token prompt, 2048 decoded tokens, -fa off, greedy, seed 123.
All 12 runs: rc=0, flash_attn parsed back as "disabled", 2048 tokens decoded.

All four cells are in THIS block. The one missing arm could have been run alone
and differenced against the first block's a1_nodrain, but that is exactly the
cross-block contrast the fig7 provenance note rejected. Both differences below
are within-block; re-measuring the alpha=0 pair also buys an independent
replication of the published drain cost, which turns out to matter (Finding 2).

RESULTS
-------
  condition    alpha  drains  n   mean tok/s    sd
  a0_nodrain    0.00   no     3      29.642   0.067
  a0_drain      0.00   yes    3      29.720   0.165
  a1_nodrain    1.00   no     3      29.069   0.428
  a1_drain      1.00   yes    3      28.088   0.018

  drain cost (undrained - drained) at alpha=0 :  -0.078 +/- 0.103   t = -0.76
  drain cost (undrained - drained) at alpha=1 :  +0.981 +/- 0.247   t = +3.97
  interaction (difference of the two)         :  +1.059 +/- 0.268   t = +3.96

  VERDICT: the drain cost is alpha-DEPENDENT. The decomposition is not validated.

FINDING 1: THERE IS NO FIXED DRAIN COST — THE DRAIN COSTS WHAT IT EXPOSES
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
At alpha=0 the drain is free: -0.078 +/- 0.103 tok/s, indistinguishable from
zero and if anything the wrong sign. At alpha=1 the same drain costs
+0.981 +/- 0.247 tok/s. The interaction is as large as either main effect
(main effect of alpha -1.103, of drain -0.452, interaction 1.059).

That is the physically expected result and it is worth stating as a mechanism
rather than a coefficient. synchronize() has no intrinsic price on this path;
what it does is prevent the injected CPU sleep from overlapping in-flight Metal
work. With no sleep to expose (alpha=0) it exposes nothing. With a ~3.1 ms sleep
against a ~34 ms step (alpha=1) it exposes nearly all of it. The drain and the
modeled transfer are not two costs that add; they are one mechanism observed
through a switch.

FINDING 2: THE PUBLISHED alpha=0 DRAIN COST DOES NOT REPLICATE
----------------------------------------------------------------
  block 1 (2026-08-03):  +0.197 +/- 0.177  t = +1.11
  block 2 (this block):  -0.078 +/- 0.103  t = -0.76

The published +0.20 tok/s was never statistically distinguishable from zero in
its own block (t = 1.11), and this block gets the opposite sign. The blocks agree
on levels to 0.4-0.5% on all three shared conditions (a0_nodrain 29.642 vs
29.762, a0_drain 29.720 vs 29.566, a1_nodrain 29.069 vs 29.221), so this is not
a calibration difference between blocks — the alpha=0 drain cost is simply
smaller than the block-to-block variation. Pooling both blocks, the best estimate
of the drain cost at alpha=0 is zero.

The "29%" therefore has no measured referent.

FINDING 3: THE ADDITIVITY ARGUMENT IN THE FIRST BLOCK WAS A TELESCOPING SUM
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
This is the part that needs correcting rather than merely extending, and the
first block's own data was already sufficient to show it.

drain_control_analysis.txt argues the effects "ARE additive once the drain is
held constant" because 0.20 + 0.50 = 0.70 against 0.69 measured together. That
sum is a tautology. Walking any path across a 2x2 from one corner to the
opposite corner sums to the corner-to-corner difference by construction. Using
the same four block-1 cell means:

  corners: (a=0,nodrain) 29.762   (a=0,drain) 29.566
           (a=.25,nodrain) 29.781 (a=.25,drain) 29.068

  published path, drain first  : drain +0.197, then transfer +0.498  sum +0.694
  other path, transfer first   : transfer -0.019, then drain +0.713  sum +0.694

Both sum to +0.694. Additivity requires the two paths to agree on each TERM, and
they do not: the drain costs +0.197 at alpha=0 and +0.713 at alpha=0.25, and the
transfer costs -0.019 undrained and +0.498 drained. The within-block interaction
in block 1 alone is +0.516 +/- 0.190, t = +2.72 — significant, present in the
published data, and not computed at the time. This block confirms it
independently at a wider alpha spacing (+1.059, t = +3.96).

Two independent blocks, two different alpha ranges, same conclusion.

DRAIN COST AS A FUNCTION OF ALPHA — EVERY POINT WITHIN ITS OWN BLOCK
---------------------------------------------------------------------
  alpha    drain cost (undrained - drained)      block
  0.00     +0.197 +/- 0.177   (t =  1.11, n.s.)  1
  0.00     -0.078 +/- 0.103   (t = -0.76, n.s.)  2
  0.25     +0.713 +/- 0.068   (t = 10.46)        1
  1.00     +0.981 +/- 0.247   (t =  3.97)        2

Monotone and consistent across blocks: zero when there is no delay to expose,
clearly positive once there is. The two blocks are listed separately and must not
be fitted jointly — that would be the splice fig7 declined.

THE POSITION CONFOUND, AND WHY IT DOES NOT EXPLAIN THE RESULT
---------------------------------------------------------------
Reported because it is a real defect in this block's design. The shuffle drew a
bad layout: alpha=1 conditions landed early and alpha=0 late (mean slots 3.00,
5.00, 7.33, 10.67 for a1_nodrain, a1_drain, a0_drain, a0_nodrain). Condition is
confounded with block position, and worse, the two drain contrasts run in
OPPOSITE directions with respect to position — so a monotone thermal drift would
bias them oppositely and could manufacture exactly this interaction. This is the
failure the B2 brief warned about: randomization alone does not guarantee
position balance, and this harness relied on it alone. A future block should
position-balance explicitly.

Three checks say the draw was harmless here:

1. THERE IS NO DRIFT. Removing condition means and regressing the residuals on
   slot gives +0.0009 tok/s per slot (se 0.0165, t = 0.05) — +0.009 tok/s across
   the entire block. The 200 s cooldowns did their job. The drift-implied bias in
   the interaction is -0.005 tok/s against a measured 1.059.
2. STRICTLY LOCAL CONTRASTS, immune to any monotone drift, give the same answer.
   At alpha=1, a1_nodrain sits in slot 6 between a1_drain in slots 5 and 7:
   29.016 - 28.096 = +0.919. At alpha=0, the adjacent pairs 8/9 and 10/11 give
   +0.010 and -0.226, mean -0.108. Local interaction: +1.027.
3. DETRENDED cell means give +1.064.

Full-block +1.059, local +1.027, detrended +1.064. The finding is not an artifact
of the layout.

One honest caveat on precision: a1_nodrain is much noisier than the other three
cells (sd 0.428 against 0.067, 0.165, 0.018) and its standard error dominates the
interaction's. Dropping its two earliest runs and using only its coolest slot
gives an alpha=1 drain cost of +0.928 instead of +0.981, so the noise affects the
third digit, not the conclusion. Why that one cell is noisy is not established
here — it is the cell where a large injected sleep is left to overlap, so partial
absorption may genuinely vary run to run.

WHAT THE PAPER SHOULD SAY
--------------------------
The 71/29 split should be withdrawn, not restated with a different pair of
numbers, because there is no alpha at which it is a decomposition into two
separable costs. The replacement claim is stronger and simpler, and it is what
both blocks actually show:

  The pipeline drain has no measurable cost of its own (alpha=0: t = 1.11 and
  t = -0.76 in two independent blocks). Its cost is the injected delay it stops
  the pipeline from absorbing, and it therefore grows with alpha: +0.71 tok/s at
  alpha=0.25 and +0.98 at alpha=1. Drain and modeled transfer are one mechanism
  seen through a switch, not two additive terms.

This does NOT rescue the alpha=0 offset from the reviewer's objection, and the
paper should not present it as if it did. Comparing like with like at drain OFF,
the alpha=0 -> alpha=0.25 step is +0.019 +/- 0.174 — nothing. The whole step
exists only in the drained comparison, which is the comparison whose
instrumentation changes at the treatment boundary. The measured content of the
alpha model at alpha=0.25 is: an injected delay that a drained pipeline pays in
full and an undrained one does not pay at all.

WHAT IS UNAFFECTED
------------------
- fig7 and artifacts/drain_control/fig7_provenance.txt. Every value fig7 plots is
  a cell mean from block 1, and its headline is the within-block alpha=0.25 gap
  (+0.713 +/- 0.068, t = 10.5), which stands. The figure draws two series with
  different slopes; the interaction reported here is that difference in slopes,
  so the figure is if anything a more direct picture of the corrected claim than
  of the one it was drawn for. No regeneration needed.
- The recall-cost fit, the capacity findings, and the token-identity result.
- The alpha sweep's drained slope (-1.197 +/- 0.052), which was always measured
  with the drain on throughout and is internally consistent.

LIMITS
------
- One machine, one model, C=1024, 512-token prompt, 2048 decoded tokens. Drain
  cost is a function of how much asynchronous work is in flight, which is
  workload-specific.
- Two alpha values in this block. "Grows with alpha" is supported by four points
  across two blocks; the SHAPE of that growth is not measured and should not be
  fitted.
- The alpha=0 conclusion is a failure to reject, from n=3 in each of two blocks.
  It bounds the drain cost at roughly +/-0.2 tok/s (0.7% of throughput), not to
  exactly zero.
