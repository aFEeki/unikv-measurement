The constant-work thermal control — the bound becomes a measurement, and the
heat turns out to sit in gamma, not delta
================================================================================
Run 2026-08-20 10:11-15:55. Harness: scripts/run_thermal_control.py
Analysis: scripts/analyze_thermal_control.py
Data:    stress_results/thermal_control_block.csv (72 runs, 3 complete rounds)
Console: artifacts/thermal_control/block.log   Logs: artifacts/thermal_control/logs/
All rc=0, flash attention parsed back as "disabled" on all 72, zero flags,
no exclusions.

HEADLINE
--------
Thermal drift is real and measurable — a constant-work burst slows by 0.5 to
1.2 ms per step depending on how long the machine has been under load. But it is
very nearly CONSTANT across spill targets rather than proportional to n_spill,
so it lands on the intercept, not the slope.

  delta, thermal share:   CPU-pinned 2.27%    device-visible -0.09%

Both are well under the ~6% the paper's arithmetic bounds the residual at. The
bound was correct in direction and conservative in size, and the two per-cell
terms can now be reported as ESTIMATES rather than upper bounds.

  gamma, thermal share:   CPU-pinned -6.0%    device-visible -20.8%

That is where the heat actually was. The fixed two-tier entry cost is
contaminated by drift, materially so on the device-visible tier.

WHY THE CONTROL HAD TO BE MATCHED ON ELAPSED WALL-CLOCK, NOT BURST DURATION
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
The brief specified burst duration. The timeline says that would have controlled
almost none of the heat. Measured from the published isochronal block's own step
logs, the bursts barely differ across targets — 2.17 s to 5.51 s — while the
PROCESS durations run 4.7 s to 94.3 s, because the prefill that establishes the
spilled tier dominates everything else. At CPU-pinned n_spill=8192 roughly 89 of
the 94 seconds precede the measurement window.

So each control is matched to the elapsed time at which its paired isochronal
run's window opens and closes:

  mode             target   band [T_start, T_end] s
  CPU-pinned            0   [ 2.5,  4.7]
  CPU-pinned          512   [ 6.5,  9.8]
  CPU-pinned         1024   [ 9.0, 12.3]
  CPU-pinned         2048   [15.4, 19.0]
  CPU-pinned         4096   [33.4, 37.6]
  CPU-pinned         8192   [88.8, 94.3]
  device-visible        0   [ 2.5,  4.7]
  device-visible      512   [ 5.2,  7.7]
  device-visible     1024   [ 6.9,  9.6]
  device-visible     2048   [10.4, 13.3]
  device-visible     4096   [18.7, 22.0]
  device-visible     8192   [41.4, 45.5]

These are DESIGN CONSTANTS from the published block, used only to decide how long
each control runs. They appear in no arithmetic.

NO SPLICING: THE ISOCHRONAL ARMS WERE RE-RUN HERE
---------------------------------------------------
Correcting the published isochronal block with a new control would have been a
cross-block subtraction of exactly the kind the rules forbid. So this block
contains BOTH kinds — 12 configurations x {isochronal, control} = 24 cells,
three rounds, randomised complete block, each round a random permutation of all
24 cells so kind and target are orthogonal to position by construction. The
correction is paired WITHIN round:

  thermal(dev, target, round) = ctrl_ms(dev, target, round) - ctrl_ms(dev, 0, round)
  corrected(run)              = iso_ms(run) - thermal(dev, target, round of run)

Both kinds instrumented (UNIKV_LOG), as the published isochronal block was, so
the instrumentation synchronize is common-mode and cancels in the subtraction.

CONTROL PROMPT: 1000 tokens at C=1024, so the window is full after 24 generated
tokens (~0.7 s) and every band — the earliest opens at 2.5 s — sits in the
constant-work regime. A 512-token prompt, the obvious choice, would have put the
earliest band inside the still-growing-cache regime and silently broken the
control by making the reference cheaper for a non-thermal reason.

------------------------------------

Two things to read off this. First, the drift is REAL: up to 1.16 ms on a
~25.7 ms step, 4.5%. Anyone assuming a cooled block is thermally flat within a
run is wrong. Second, and this is the finding, the drift is not proportional to
n_spill. It jumps almost at once — half of the CPU-pinned drift is already
present at target 512 — and then rises slowly and non-monotonically. On the
device-visible tier it is essentially flat and its ordering is noise.

A near-constant offset across targets shifts the intercept of a fit against
n_spill and leaves the slope alone. That is precisely what the corrected fit
shows.

WHAT THE PAPER SHOULD SAY
--------------------------
  "A constant-work control matched on elapsed time under load measures the
  thermal contribution directly. Drift is real — up to 1.16 ms on a 25.7 ms step
  at the longest timeline — but it is near-constant across spilled-set sizes
  rather than proportional to them, so it inflates the fixed term and not the
  per-cell term. Correcting for it moves delta by 2.3% (CPU-pinned) and -0.1%
  (device-visible), inside the 6% the arithmetic bounded it at, and the per-cell
  terms are therefore reported as estimates: 3.084 and 2.197 us/cell. The fixed
  term is more affected: gamma falls 6.0% and 20.8%, and the device-visible
  reduction in gamma rises from 69.9% to 74.7%."

The last clause is a strengthening, not a weakness: correcting for heat makes the
device-visible tier look BETTER on the fixed term, because the CPU-pinned arm's
longer timeline was inflating both arms and the device-visible gamma is small
enough that a ~0.57 ms offset was a fifth of it.

THE COST OF THE CORRECTION, STATED PLAINLY
--------------------------------------------
Subtracting a measured quantity imports that quantity's sampling error. The
corrected slopes are less precise than the raw ones:

    CPU-pinned      slope SE 0.0633 -> 0.0723 us/cell  (+14%)
    device-visible  slope SE 0.0091 -> 0.0165 us/cell  (+82%)

and residual SD rises correspondingly (0.685 -> 0.782, 0.098 -> 0.179). The
device-visible arm is hit hardest because its raw fit was extraordinarily tight
and the control's noise is large relative to it. So the corrected numbers are
better centred and worse determined, and the honest presentation gives both. It
does not change any conclusion: the slopes remain separable at t = 11.96.

LIMITS
------
- The control matches ELAPSED TIME UNDER LOAD, not power draw. Policy 1 at a
  pinned window and policy 3 with a large spilled tier do not dissipate
  identically, so this measures the drift a constant-work burst of the same
  duration would see — the best available proxy, not a thermal equivalence.
- The drift's SHAPE across targets is noisy (sd up to 0.42 ms, and non-monotone
  on both tiers). The claim supported is "near-constant, not proportional to
  n_spill", which is what the fit needs; the individual per-target drift figures
  should not be quoted as precise.
- One model, one machine, C=1024, three rounds.
- The band times come from the published block's timeline. If a future block's
  runs are materially faster or slower, the bands would need recomputing.
