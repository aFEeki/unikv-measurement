F4 / A2 — continuation policies (and small-ubatch upstream) at the 65536-token workload
=======================================================================================
Run 2026-08-05. Harness: scripts/run_f4_phaseA.py (UNIKV_F4=A2)
Data: stress_results/f4_a2_continuation_arms.csv
Logs: artifacts/f4_phaseA/logs/gen_A2_*.txt
Maps to: Finding 4 (capacity limits appear at execution).

*** SECOND CONTRADICTION OF THE SAME CLAIM. Combined with A3, Finding 4's
    "Beyond the ceiling" paragraph needs rewriting, not qualifying. ***

QUESTION
--------
Finding 4 asserts, without measuring, that the continuation policies "would also
complete this workload at a small C by evicting, discarding most of the context
in the process", and concludes that "what is unavailable at any upstream setting
is completing it losslessly". Measure the first half. And, prompted by A1's
finding that ubatch moves the ceiling by 1.7x, test whether a small-ubatch
upstream configuration can hold the whole prompt on the non-fused path.

RESULT — ALL THREE ARMS COMPLETE
---------------------------------
65536-token prompt + 32 decoded tokens, flash attention off on every arm
(verified per run from its own log), uninstrumented.

  arm                          C      ubatch  device MiB  xbudget  prefill s  decode tok/s
  rolling window (p1)          4096      512      5481.3    0.335      213.8        27.68
  H2O (p4)                     4096      512      5737.8    0.350      293.2        31.63
  stock upstream (p0)         81920       64     15587.8    0.951      614.4         5.86

The first two confirm the asserted claim: both continuation policies complete the
workload at C=4096, discarding all but 4096 tokens of context, and both are far
faster than exact retention's 1.50 tok/s.

The third is the one that matters. Stock upstream, flash attention OFF, ubatch
64, C=81920 COMPLETES the workload -- and because C > 65536, it holds the ENTIRE
prompt. It is not discarding anything. It is lossless.

WHERE THE 65536-TOKEN WORKLOAD NOW STANDS
------------------------------------------
  configuration                        device MiB  decode tok/s  lossless?
  exact retention, C=4096 + host tier      5481.3          1.50  yes
  rolling window, C=4096                   5481.3         27.68  no
  H2O, C=4096                              5737.8         31.63  no
  upstream fa-off, ubatch 64, C=81920     15587.8          5.86  yes (whole prompt)
  upstream fa-on,  ubatch 512, C=73728    14159.8         15.99  yes (whole prompt)

Exact retention is the SLOWEST way to complete this workload losslessly. Two
upstream configurations also complete it losslessly, at 3.9x and 10.7x its decode
throughput. The claim "no upstream setting runs it" is false on the fused path
(A3) and false on the non-fused path (here), so it is false as stated under any
scoping.

WHAT SURVIVES, AND IT IS STILL A REAL RESULT
---------------------------------------------
The advantage is device footprint at a given context length, not feasibility:

  exact retention completes the 65536-token workload with a 5481 MiB device
  working set, 2.8x smaller than the smallest upstream configuration that also
  completes it losslessly (15588 MiB, fa-off ubatch 64) and 2.6x smaller than
  the fastest one (14160 MiB, fa-on). It pays 3.9x-10.7x in decode throughput
  for that.

That is a defensible sentence and it is the one the paragraph should make. It is
a memory/throughput trade, measured, with the trade stated in both directions. It
is NOT a feasibility result.

HOW THE FEASIBILITY CLAIM COULD BE RESTORED (not run)
------------------------------------------------------
The fused upstream ceiling is the binding one: scratch is constant at 258.5 MiB
with flash attention on, so upstream tops out near
(16383 - 4685 - 258) MiB / 128 KiB ~= 91000 tokens. A workload above that -- say
a 120000-token prompt -- would have no upstream setting on either path, and exact
retention at C=4096 would in principle still run it.

Not attempted here. Extrapolating the measured spilled-tier prefill scaling
(~N^2.4: 16384 tokens in 229 s, 65536 in 6364 s) puts a 120000-token prefill near
7 hours, which is an overnight run and a Phase-B-scale decision rather than a
Phase A item. It is also worth asking first whether a feasibility claim that
requires a 7-hour prefill is one the paper wants to make.

LIMITS
------
- Single runs per arm; outcomes are binary (completes / refused) and the tok/s
  figures are single uninstrumented runs quoted as approximate, not block means.
- The small-ubatch upstream arm buys its capacity with prefill parallelism:
  614 s against 494 s for the fa-on arm at a larger ubatch. Lower ubatch is a
  real lever on the ceiling and a real cost, and neither should be reported
  without the other.
- One device, one model, one quantisation.
