Recall cost vs spilled-set size — summary
=========================================
Run 2026-08-04. Addresses review items M7 ("the recall cost is characterized by
two points"), P5 ("measure out to 10^4 or drop the agentic motivation"), and
feeds the compute-cost term D3/C1 asks for in the cost model.
Numeric detail: artifacts/recall_cost/recall_cost_analysis.txt
Data: stress_results/recall_cost_isochronal.csv        (controlled, PRIMARY)
      stress_results/recall_cost_steps_A_p3_long.csv   (single long run)
      stress_results/recall_cost_steps_B_p1_ctrl.csv   (thermal control)
      stress_results/recall_cost_e2e.csv               (uninstrumented e2e)
Figure: figures/fig5_recall_cost.pdf/.png

QUESTION
--------
The paper supports "the recurring recall cost grows with the spilled set" with
two points (33.0 tok/s at 511 cells, 28.5 at 1535). What is the actual shape,
and does it survive extrapolation to the 10^4-10^5-token contexts the
discussion invokes?

THE MEASUREMENT TRAP (and why there are three datasets)
-------------------------------------------------------
The obvious experiment — one long decode run, plot ms/step against n_spill —
does not work on this device. n_spill grows monotonically with elapsed time, and
this machine droops ~21% over ~250 s, so n_spill and temperature are the same
variable and no within-run regression can separate them.

  (1) Single long run (A): C=1024, 512-token prompt, 10752-token decode budget,
      reaching n_spill = 10239. Per-step cost rises 33 -> 83 ms. Raw slope
      5.32 us/cell.
  (2) Thermal control (B): policy 1, same C and budget, constant work per step
      (window pinned at 1024, nothing spilled). Its droop is +6.0 ms/step over
      303 s (+21.3%), confirming the magnitude. Subtracting it linearly gives
      4.18 us/cell — but its droop SATURATES around 240 s while run A lasts
      610 s, and it is a GPU-only workload while policy 3 is CPU-heavy, so this
      correction is an approximation and under-corrects.
  (3) Controlled / isochronal (PRIMARY): the spilled tier is established during
      PREFILL to a target size, then a short decode burst is timed. n_spill is
      fixed before the burst instead of growing through it. Randomized-block
      order, cooldowns between runs.

  including n=4096:

THE SHAPE, AND WHY IT MATTERS
------------------------------
The cost is NOT dominated by the size of the spilled set over the measured
range. It splits into two very different terms:

  fixed:     entering the two-tier path at all costs ~9.0 ms/step
             (23.22 -> 32.25 ms), paid as soon as ONE cell has spilled
  marginal:  ~3.3 us per spilled cell thereafter

The marginal term does not equal the fixed term until n_spill ~ 2750 cells. So
at C=1024 with a few hundred cells spilled, essentially the entire recall cost
is a per-step constant of THIS IMPLEMENTATION — the CPU-pinned spilled matmuls,
their graph nodes, and the concat plus single softmax over the union — and
almost none of it is the retained data being large.

That is a direct, measured handle on review item P2 (the throughput cost is
plausibly an artifact of pinning the spilled tier to the CPU backend rather
than of the policy): most of the cost at realistic spill sizes is a fixed
per-step penalty for leaving the GPU path, not a per-byte cost of the retained
tier. It also means the compute term D3 wants in Eq. (2) should be affine, not
linear-through-the-origin: a per-step activation cost plus a small per-entry
cost, which is a strictly stronger (and more testable) statement than "cost
grows with the spilled set".

EXTRAPOLATION (P5) — THE LINEAR FIT DOES NOT EXTRAPOLATE. P5 WAS RIGHT.
------------------------------------------------------------------------
An earlier draft of this file extrapolated the linear fit to 10^5 cells and
called the 10^4 extrapolation "interpolation-adjacent". The capacity
demonstration (artifacts/capacity_probe/, run afterwards) measured a point at
n_spill = 61471 and falsified that:

  n_spill    linear fit      MEASURED        fit error
     1535    37.3 ms/step    --              (inside the fitted range)
     4096    45.7 ms/step    50.9 ms/step    fit low by 10%
     8192    59.2 ms/step    59.3 ms/step    exact (fitted point)
    10240    65.9 ms/step    81-83 ms/step   fit low by ~25% (long run,
                                             heat-soaked, so an upper bound)
    61471   234.4 ms/step    666 ms/step     fit low by 184%
   100000   361.2 ms/step    --              would be badly wrong

The 61471-cell point comes from the capacity demo's UniKV arm (65536-token
prompt, C=4096, UNINSTRUMENTED, so it carries LESS overhead than the
instrumented controlled points and the gap cannot be an instrumentation
artifact). Decode there ran at 1.502 tok/s.

Fitting a power law through the two ends of the measured range (8192 -> 61471,
59.3 -> 666 ms) gives an exponent of ~1.20: the per-step cost grows somewhat
FASTER than the size of the spilled set. On that form n_spill = 10^5 lands near
1200 ms/step (~0.8 tok/s), not the 361 ms/step the linear fit predicts.

Honest statement for the paper: the cost is affine and well-behaved WITHIN the
range where the fit was measured (n_spill <= ~8k), and superlinear beyond it. Do
not extrapolate the linear form past ~10^4. Where a 10^4-10^5 number is needed,
quote the measured 61471-cell point (1.5 tok/s) and say plainly that the trend
steepens. This is exactly P5's objection, and the answer is that the agentic
motivation does NOT survive linear extrapolation — it survives only as "the
policy still completes, at single-digit tokens per second".

E2E CROSS-CHECK (uninstrumented wall clock, randomized block, n=2 each)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
  final n_spill   mean tok/s   sd
           1024        31.53  0.07
           2560        26.85  0.30
           5632        20.85  0.62
An e2e run averages over n_spill = 0..final, so these necessarily sit above the
per-step cost at their final n_spill; they test the shape, not the level. Run
A's own prefixes reproduce the two shorter ones to within 2% (31.77 vs 31.53 at
1536 tokens; 27.30 vs 26.85 at 3072).

BONUS: PREFILL COST WHEN THE TIER IS BUILT DURING INGESTION
------------------------------------------------------------
  prompt   512 tok :   1.19 s (430 tok/s)      prompt  3072 tok : 13.06 s (235 tok/s)
  prompt  1536 tok :   4.53 s (339 tok/s)      prompt  5120 tok : 29.42 s (174 tok/s)
  prompt  2048 tok :   7.03 s (291 tok/s)      prompt  9216 tok : 83.41 s (110 tok/s)
Growth is ~quadratic: every ubatch attends over the whole spilled tier, on the
CPU. This is what sizes any long-context experiment on this build — a 65k-token
prompt extrapolates to roughly an hour of prefill.

ALSO OBSERVED: A ONE-OFF GRAPH-REALLOCATION STEP
-------------------------------------------------
The first decode step after a batched prefill costs ~8 s at n_spill=8192 (vs a
~68 ms steady state), scaling with the spilled-tier size (~1.2 s at n_spill=0,
~4.7 s at 4096). It is the ggml graph being rebuilt when the ubatch shape drops
from 512 to 1 with a large spilled tier attached. Recorded per run in the
`transient_ms` column and EXCLUDED from the per-step figures above (the first 32
burst steps are dropped). It is a real user-visible latency at the
prompt-to-generation boundary and worth a sentence in the paper, but it is not
part of the steady-state recall cost.

LIMITS
------
- n=2 per controlled point (n=5 at 4096). Every point repeats to under 0.5 ms
  EXCEPT n=4096, whose 5 trials spread 44.0-60.9 ms (sd 7.0). Unexplained; not
  trimmed. The fit is reported with and without it and the slope moves 3.29 ->
  3.41 us/cell, so the conclusion does not turn on it.
- The controlled design still self-heats: a larger target needs a longer prefill
  immediately before the burst (83 s at n_spill=8192). Larger points are
  therefore measured warmer than smaller ones, which if anything inflates the
  slope — so 3.3 us/cell is an upper bound on the cool-device marginal cost.
- One model, one quantization, one C. The fixed/marginal split is specific to
  this CPU-pinned two-tier implementation; a GPU-side spilled tier (P2) would be
  expected to change the fixed term drastically and is the experiment that would
  settle whether the ~9 ms is inherent or incidental.
